\section{Introduction}
\begin{frame}{Carbon Pricing's Effect on Electricity Markets}
    \begin{itemize}
    \item We want to understand how carbon prices impact firm offer behaviour in electricity markets
    \item Electricity markets, at least in certain jurisdictions, are ideal environments in which to study responses to carbon prices
    \item Alberta policy changes alter both carbon prices and output-based credit allocations
    \item Rich, hourly electricity market data allows us to observe firm-level decisions with respect to carbon price pass through
    \item Fabra and Reguant (2014) shows how firms respond to emissions credit prices and that lump-sum credit allocations were not distortionary in the short run
    \item Brown et al. (2018) have looked at the dynamics of such a market in a simplified, simulation model
    \end{itemize}
\vfill
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Alberta's Power Market is an Ideal Laboratory}
    \begin{itemize}
    \item Alberta's power market is relatively small, isolated, and features a mix of generation technologies
    \item Significant hourly variability in intertie capacity helps us with identification
     \item Alberta has minimal intertie capacity: WECC (1325 MW export and 1500 MW import path-rating) and Saskatchewan (150 MW path-rating)
      \item Peak internal load for 2018 was 11,697 MW, minimum internal load was 7,819 MW. Sustained low prices prevailed from 2015 through 2018.
    \item Supply mix (2018) was 45\% coal, 21\% net-to-grid from combined heat and power plants, 19\% natural gas, 3\% hydro, 6\% wind, 1\% other sources including solar and 5\% imports.
  \end{itemize}
\vfill
\end{frame}

\section{Discussion}

\begin{frame}{Discussion}
   \begin{itemize}
   \item Alberta's energy-only power market provides an ideal environment to study carbon price pass-through
    \item Alberta policy changes alter both carbon prices and output-based credit allocations provide facility-specific treatment
    \item Preliminary results show that policy changes in 2016, 2017, and 2018 saw significant changes in offers from coal and natural gas combined cycle plants
    \item Framework allows us to examine changes in firm, offer control, fuel or other dimensions as well as market power implications
    \item Machine-learning-based estimation of synthetic offer curves due to market conditions still to come
    \item Glass half-full: impact of new policies and other proposed changes might provide an additional treatment.  
    \end{itemize}
   \vfill
\end{frame}
